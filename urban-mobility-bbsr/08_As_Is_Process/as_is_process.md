# As-Is Process: Route & Frequency Planning (Inferred)

## Purpose
This document describes the *current, inferred* process by which bus route and frequency decisions likely happen at CRUT today, based on general knowledge of Indian STU (State Transport Undertaking) operations and publicly available information about CRUT. It is not based on internal CRUT process documentation, and is explicitly marked as an inferred baseline for comparison against the proposed To-Be process.

## As-Is Process Flow

1. **Service Area Expansion Trigger**
   New areas (e.g., Cuttack, Puri extensions) are identified for coverage, typically driven by city growth, public demand, or government direction, rather than a standing analytical review cycle.

2. **Route Design (Manual/Experience-Based)**
   Routes are planned based on operational experience, known major roads, existing hubs (e.g., Baramunda ISBT), and connectivity goals — not a quantified demand model at the stop level.

3. **Bus & Driver Allocation**
   A fixed number of buses/drivers is allocated per route based on available fleet capacity, not necessarily recalculated against real-time or seasonal demand shifts.

4. **Operational Monitoring (ITS-Based)**
   CRUT's Intelligent Transport System (ITS) tracks buses in real time and can surface high-level patterns (peak times, popular routes), primarily for operational monitoring (e.g., "where are our buses right now") rather than for systematic route-level demand-frequency optimization.

5. **Ad-Hoc Adjustment**
   When issues arise (overcrowding complaints, underused routes noticed informally), frequency or route adjustments may be made reactively, on a case-by-case basis.

6. **No Standing Feedback Loop to Re-Optimize Existing Routes**
   There is no evident, publicly referenced, periodic process where the *entire existing network* is re-evaluated against a demand model, congestion data, and cost-benefit analysis together.

## Key Gaps Identified in As-Is Process
1. No stop-level or corridor-level demand quantification feeding into frequency decisions.
2. No integration between congestion/traffic data and transit frequency planning.
3. No standing cost-benefit framework to prioritize which routes deserve additional investment first.
4. Re-optimization is reactive (complaint-driven) rather than proactive (data-driven, periodic).

These gaps directly inform the To-Be Process and the Business Requirements Document.
