# Business Impact

## Purpose
This document summarizes the quantified financial and environmental impact of the recommendations in `14_Solution_Proposal.md`, and translates those figures into business-relevant terms for CRUT and BSCL decision-makers.

## Financial Impact Summary (Network-Wide, All 22 Flagged Routes)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total routes analyzed | 73 |
| Routes flagged for frequency increase | 22 |
| Total additional daily operating cost | ₹68,083.60 |
| Total additional daily revenue | ₹29,929.63 |
| **Net daily benefit (network-wide)** | **−₹38,153.97** |
| Average ROI ratio | 0.47 |

**Interpretation**: Under current assumptions, a full network-wide rollout of frequency increases across all 22 flagged routes would require an estimated **₹38,153.97/day** (≈ ₹1.39 crore/year) in operating subsidy beyond fare revenue captured. This is a common and expected pattern for public transit — most city bus networks globally operate at a fare-recovery ratio below 1.0 and rely on public subsidy, since the service's value includes public benefits (congestion reduction, emissions reduction, equitable mobility access) not captured in fare revenue alone.

## Financial Impact — Phase 1 Candidate (R_SYN002)
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total daily demand | 1,509 riders |
| Net daily benefit | −₹73 |
| ROI ratio | 0.98 |

This single route is nearly self-funding, making it the lowest-risk starting point to validate the model's assumptions in the real world before wider rollout.

## Environmental Impact Summary

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Assumed mode-shift rate (private vehicle → bus) | 20% of new riders |
| Daily CO2 saved (network-wide, flagged routes) | 179.58 kg |
| **Annual CO2 saved (network-wide, flagged routes)** | **65,545.90 kg (≈65.5 metric tons)** |

**Interpretation**: While the financial case alone shows a net operating cost, the environmental case provides an additional, quantifiable public value. Sixty-five metric tons of annual CO2 reduction is a concrete figure that can support funding applications tied to Smart City sustainability goals or state/national emissions-reduction targets, independent of the farebox financial case.

## Combined Business Case
When viewed together, the recommendation is best framed not as "this is profitable" but as: **"This investment costs approximately ₹1.39 crore/year in subsidy and delivers 65.5 metric tons/year of CO2 reduction, concentrated most efficiently on Route R_SYN002 as a low-risk pilot."** This framing is more defensible to funding stakeholders than presenting the numbers as self-funding, which they are not under current assumptions.

## Risk & Sensitivity Notes
- These figures are highly sensitive to the **15% demand-capture assumption** — if real capture is higher (e.g., due to genuinely underserved demand), the financial case improves significantly; if lower, it worsens. This is the single most important assumption to validate first via the Phase 1 pilot.
- Real CRUT operating costs may differ meaningfully from the ₹65/km reference figure used here (based on a different STU's published data), which would directly change the cost side of every calculation.
- CO2 figures depend on the 20% mode-shift assumption; if most new riders are net-new trips (not shifted from private vehicles), actual emissions benefit would be lower.

## Recommendation
Proceed with **Phase 1 validation on R_SYN002 only**, using real post-implementation ridership and cost data to recalibrate the model's assumptions before committing to a wider rollout across the remaining 21 flagged routes.
